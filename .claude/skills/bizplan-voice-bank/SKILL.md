# Bank / Lender Business Plan Voice

Modeled on how commercial credit memos are actually written and reviewed — the internal document a loan officer produces to get a loan approved, and the standard a business plan is implicitly measured against once it reaches a bank.

## How this voice writes
Terse, declarative, unembellished. Every risk named explicitly and paired immediately with what offsets it. Every figure traceable to a source. The goal isn't to excite the reader — it's to survive a credit committee's scrutiny, where the operating question is never "is this exciting?" but "can this business repay, and what happens if something goes wrong?"

## Rules

**1 — Lead with repayment capacity, not opportunity.**
The organizing question: can the business repay, will it repay, and what's the secondary repayment source if cash flow weakens? Open with this framing, not market size or vision.

**2 — Name 3–5 material risks, each with a stated mitigant.**
Not a longer list — credit reviewers want the few risks that matter, not every risk that theoretically exists. Pattern: "[Specific, quantified risk]. [What offsets it]." — e.g., customer concentration paired with contract length or a diversification plan.

**3 — Cite every figure to its source.**
"Revenue grew 18% YoY, per FY2025 tax returns" — not a bare number. An unsourced figure reads as an assumption, and assumptions get discounted.

**4 — No qualitative claim without a number attached.**
Not "strong cash flow" — the debt service coverage ratio, stated. Not "significant growth" — the percentage and the period.

**5 — Formal register: no contractions, no humor, no informal asides.**
The most formal of the three voices. Write as if the reader has never met you and needs the document to stand entirely on its own evidence.

**6 — State use of funds and repayment source in one direct sentence each.**
No elaboration needed beyond the fact itself: what the money buys, and what specifically repays it.

**7 — Address the downside directly — never omit or soften it.**
The opposite instinct from the VC voice: this one volunteers the risk rather than waiting to be asked. A lender who senses an omitted risk discounts the whole document.

## Sounds like
> The business requests $180,000 to purchase equipment that will reduce per-unit production cost by 14%. Debt service coverage on the proposed facility is 1.35x based on trailing-twelve-month cash flow. The primary risk is customer concentration: one account represents 31% of revenue. This is mitigated by a three-year supply contract signed in FY2025 and an active pipeline of two additional accounts in late-stage negotiation.

## Apply this voice when
Drafting business plan content for a bank, credit union, SBA package, or any institutional lender.
